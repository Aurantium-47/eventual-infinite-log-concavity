# Analytic part of a computer-assisted proof for powers of \(1+x+x^2\)

## 1. Statement and structure of the proof

For a finitely supported sequence \(a=(a_k)_{k\in\mathbb Z}\), extended by zero outside its support, write
\[
 (\mathcal La)_k=a_k^2-a_{k-1}a_{k+1}.
\]
The sequence is **infinitely log-concave** if every iterate \(\mathcal L^j a\) is nonnegative.

The result addressed by this draft is the following integer-exponent statement:

> **Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge10\), the coefficient sequence of \((1+x+x^2)^n\) is infinitely log-concave.

This is the trinomial case discussed in Medina–Straub, Example 5.3 and Table 1. It is not the general assertion for every polynomial with positive coefficients in their Conjecture 5.2. See [Medina–Straub, *On multiple and infinite log-concavity*](https://arminstraub.com/downloads/pub/infinitelogconcavity.pdf).

The proof is computer-assisted in two explicit ways: certain low-degree rational polynomial inequalities are verified by positive Bernstein coefficients, and a finite collection of parameter-wide sign inequalities is verified by exact interval Taylor arithmetic. Floating-point calculations may select rational evaluation points, but do not certify their signs.

Put
\[
 p(z)=1+z+z^2,\qquad a_{n,k}=[z^k]p(z)^n,
 \qquad f_{n,j}(x)=\sum_k(\mathcal L^j a_n)_k x^k.
\]
The analytic argument proves that \(f_{n,4}\) has only negative real zeros for every integer \(n\ge4004\). Its degree is \(2n\). We do not require, or assert here, this stronger real-rootedness statement throughout the smaller finite range.

The proof has three parts.

1. A uniform two-saddle estimate gives alternating signs of \(f_{n,4}\) at explicitly defined bulk phase points.
2. Exact parameter-wide certificates supply a finite chain of edge signs and its connection to the bulk.
3. The intermediate value theorem and reciprocity exhaust the degree. The initial three iterates and the finite range \(10\le n\le4003\) are treated in the companion [finite and algebraic chapter](FINITE_AND_ALGEBRAIC_PROOF.md).

Once \(f_{n,4}\) has negative real zeros, Brändén's preservation theorem applies repeatedly to the same zero-extended operator \(\mathcal L\). The theorem and its proof are in §3 of [Brändén, *Iterated sequences and the geometry of zeros*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.1927). It is used forward from the fourth iterate, not backward to infer the first three iterates.

Throughout, \(\operatorname{sinc}x=\sin(x)/x\), with its continuous value at zero. Arguments of analytic functions are chosen continuously from the positive real axis whenever this is specified.

## 2. An exact sixteen-leaf integral

### 2.1. The diagonal identity

Consider a complete balanced binary tree with \(r=2^j\) labelled leaves. For an internal node \(\nu\), let \(L_\nu,R_\nu\) be its two child leaf sets, and put
\[
 Z_{\nu,L}=\prod_{i\in L_\nu}z_i,
 \qquad Z_{\nu,R}=\prod_{i\in R_\nu}z_i.
\]
Define
\[
 P_j(z)=\prod_{\nu}\left(1-\frac{Z_{\nu,L}}{Z_{\nu,R}}\right).
\]
Then
\[
 f_{n,j}(x)=\operatorname{CT}_{z_1,\ldots,z_{r-1}}
 P_j(z)\prod_{i=1}^r p(z_i)^n,
 \qquad z_r=\frac{x}{z_1\cdots z_{r-1}}.                 \tag{2.1}
\]
To prove this, start with the coefficientwise product formula for a diagonal. At one application of \(\mathcal L\), the product of two equal-index coefficients is replaced by that product minus the product with indices shifted by \(-1,+1\). These shifts insert exactly \(Z_{\nu,L}/Z_{\nu,R}\). Induction down the binary tree proves (2.1), including zero extension at the boundaries.

For \(x=-s^{16}\), take all sixteen radii equal to \(s\), and write \(z_i=s e^{i\alpha_i}\). The angular constraint is
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^{16}\alpha_i=\pi\pmod {2\pi}.
\]
The measure is normalized Haar measure on this fifteen-dimensional torus. In the coordinates \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{15}\), it is \((2\pi)^{-15}d\alpha_1\cdots d\alpha_{15}\).

### 2.2. A nonnegative kernel

For every internal node set
\[
 D_\nu(\alpha)=\sum_{i\in L_\nu}\alpha_i-
                    \sum_{i\in R_\nu}\alpha_i.
\]
The exponential factor \(\prod_i p(s e^{i\alpha_i})^n\) and the constrained measure are invariant under permutations of the leaves. Average the kernel over the group generated by interchanging the two children at each tree node. Beginning at the lowest level, an interchange changes \(D_\nu\) to \(-D_\nu\), while the already averaged descendant factors are even and merely permuted. It therefore replaces \(1-e^{iD_\nu}\) by \(1-\cos D_\nu\). Repeating upward gives the exact integral identity
\[
 f_{n,4}(-s^{16})=
 \int_{\sum\alpha_i=\pi\ (2\pi)}
 K(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{16}p(s e^{i\alpha_i})^n\,d\mu(\alpha),
 \qquad K=\prod_\nu(1-\cos D_\nu)\ge0.                \tag{2.2}
\]
The replacement is an equality after integration; it is not a pointwise equality with the original Laurent kernel.

At a configuration of equal angles, the leading homogeneous term of \(K\) is
\[
 H(\eta)=\prod_\nu\frac{D_\nu(\eta)^2}{2}.           \tag{2.3}
\]
There are fifteen internal nodes, so this vanishing has degree thirty.

### 2.3. Haar coordinates and measure normalization

On the tangent space
\[
 \mathcal T=\{\eta\in\mathbb R^{16}:\sum_i\eta_i=0\},
\]
the fifteen contrast vectors defining \(D_\nu\) are orthogonal. Their squared norms are the node sizes \(m_\nu\). Sizes \(2,4,8,16\) occur respectively \(8,4,2,1\) times. For orthonormal coordinates \(x_\nu=D_\nu/\sqrt{m_\nu}\),
\[
 H=\left(\prod_\nu\frac{m_\nu}{2}\right)\prod_\nu x_\nu^2
   =2048\prod_\nu x_\nu^2.                            \tag{2.4}
\]
The original fifteen angular coordinates have metric matrix \(I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\), of determinant sixteen. Thus their measure is one quarter of Euclidean measure on \(\mathcal T\). This factor is important in every saddle and complement estimate below.

## 3. Saddle geometry and explicit scalar inequalities

Set
\[
 \theta=\frac\pi{16},\quad \zeta=s e^{i\theta},\quad
 q(z)=1+4z+z^2,\quad
 m(z)=\frac{zp'(z)}{p(z)},\quad
 v(z)=\frac{zq(z)}{p(z)^2},\quad V=n v(s).
\]
For \(m\ge2\), define
\[
 \kappa_m(z)=\left(z\frac d{dz}\right)^{m-2}v(z).
\]
Here \(v(s)>0\) for \(s>0\). We work with \(0<s\le1\); reciprocity later supplies the other half of the zeros.

### 3.1. Modulus estimates

Write
\[
 h_s(\alpha)=\log\frac{|p(s e^{i\alpha})|}{p(s)}.
\]
If \(c=\cos\alpha\), the exact identity is
\[
 \frac{|p(s e^{i\alpha})|^2}{p(s)^2}=1-D(c),
 \quad D(c)=2a(1-c)+b(1-c^2),
\]
where
\[
 a=\frac{s(1+s^2)}{p(s)^2},\qquad
 b=\frac{4s^2}{p(s)^2},\qquad a+b=v(s),\qquad b\le\frac23v(s).
                                                               \tag{3.1}
\]
In particular, if \(|\alpha|\ge\pi/3\),
\[
 h_s(\alpha)\le-\frac5{12}v(s).                       \tag{3.2}
\]
A small-angle bound at the equal-phase saddle is
\[
 16h_s(\theta)>-\frac{40}{123}v(s).                  \tag{3.3}
\]
For example, \(D(\cos\theta)\le v(s)\theta^2\), \(v(s)\le1\), and \(-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)\) give
\[
 -16h_s(\theta)\le
 \frac{\pi^2}{32(1-\pi^2/256)}v(s)<\frac{40}{123}v(s).
\]

On the central interval,
\[
 \Re v(s e^{i\alpha})\ge\frac13v(s),
 \qquad |\alpha|\le\frac\pi3.                       \tag{3.4}
\]
Consequently \(h_s''(\alpha)=-\Re v(s e^{i\alpha})\le-v(s)/3\): the function is strictly concave there.

These facts locate the two modulus maxima. A configuration containing a noncentral angle has total modulus loss at least \(5v(s)/12\), larger than the equal-phase loss in (3.3). If all angles are central, the possible winding sums are \(\pm\pi,\pm3\pi,\pm5\pi\). Jensen's inequality shows that the largest modulus in each such sector occurs at its equal-angle configuration. Since \(h_s\) is even and strictly decreasing on \((0,\pi/3]\), the global maxima are exactly the two configurations with all angles equal to \(\theta\) or \(-\theta\).

### 3.2. Scalar certificate lemma

The following bounds hold uniformly for \(0<s\le1\). Put \(v_0=v(s)\), \(\beta=\Re v(\zeta)\).
\[
 \frac{19}{20}v_0\le\beta\le\frac{17}{16}v_0,
 \qquad \left(\frac{|v(\zeta)|}{\beta}\right)^{45/2}<2,             \tag{3.5}
\]
\[
 |\Im\kappa_3(\zeta)|\le\frac14v_0,
 \quad |\kappa_3(\zeta)|\le\frac65v_0,
 \quad |\kappa_4(\zeta)|\le\frac43v_0,
 \quad |\kappa_5(\zeta)|\le\frac94v_0.                            \tag{3.6}
\]
On the wider sector \(\cos\alpha\ge3/4\),
\[
 \Re\kappa_4(s e^{i\alpha})\le\frac32v_0,
 \quad |\kappa_4(s e^{i\alpha})|\le3v_0,
 \quad |\kappa_6(s e^{i\alpha})|\le32v_0.                        \tag{3.7}
\]

These are rational polynomial inequalities, rather than numerical bounds on a grid. Here is an explicit way to verify them. If
\(\kappa_m(z)=N_m(z)/p(z)^m\), then
\[
 N_2=zq(z),\qquad N_{m+1}=z\{pN_m'-m p'N_m\}.                   \tag{3.8}
\]
For a polynomial \(N(z)=\sum a_i z^i\),
\[
 |N(s e^{i\alpha})|^2
 =\sum_{i,j}a_i a_j s^{i+j}T_{|i-j|}(\cos\alpha),               \tag{3.9}
\]
where \(T_j\) is a Chebyshev polynomial. Clearing positive denominators in each squared modulus bound produces a polynomial in \(s,c\). On the narrow sector, use \(c\in[49/50,1]\), containing \(\cos\theta\), and split \(s\in[0,1]\) into four equal intervals. On the wide sector use \(c\in[3/4,1]\). Nonnegative tensor-product Bernstein controls verify the required inequalities. For a power-basis polynomial \(\sum a_{ij}x^iy^j\) of bidegree \((d,e)\), the controls are explicitly
\[
 b_{kl}=\sum_{i\le k,\,j\le l}
 a_{ij}\frac{\binom{k}{i}}{\binom d i}
        \frac{\binom l j}{\binom e j}.                           \tag{3.10}
\]
Thus the verification uses rational arithmetic only.

For completeness, some especially useful reductions are small. Let \(t=s+s^{-1}\), \(c=\cos\alpha\),
\[
 A=tc+1,\quad B^2=(t^2-4)(1-c^2),\quad D=A^2+B^2,
 \quad R=(A+3)A^2+(A-3)B^2.
\]
Then \(\Re v=R/D^2\) and \(v(s)=(t+4)/(t+1)^2\). The polynomial
\[
 R(t+1)^2-\tfrac13(t+4)D^2
\]
has positive Bernstein controls after \(t=2+u\), \(c=(1+y)/2\): the minima by powers \(u^0,\ldots,u^5\) are
\[
 148,\quad347/3,\quad65/3,\quad1/2,\quad2/3,\quad1/6.
\]
This proves (3.4). The conditioning inequality follows by verifying
\(16(\Im v)^2<(\Re v)^2\); hence
\((|v|/\Re v)^{45/2}<(17/16)^{45/4}<2\).
The lower real-part bound in (3.5) and the bounds involving real or imaginary parts in (3.6)–(3.7) are verified after the same denominator clearing, using \(t\ge2\) where convenient. The upper real-part bound also follows directly by integrating \(|\kappa_4|\le3v_0\) twice from angle zero:
\[
 \beta\le v_0(1+3\theta^2/2)<17v_0/16.
\]
The accompanying exact certificate routines specify all polynomial subdivisions; their role is confined to these finite rational inequalities.

## 4. Leading amplitude and first correction

Near the positive saddle write \(\alpha_i=\theta+\eta_i\), \(\eta\in\mathcal T\). The exponent has quadratic part
\[
 -\frac{n v(\zeta)}2\sum_i\eta_i^2.
\]
Using (2.4), the complex Gaussian integral is a product of fifteen one-dimensional second-moment integrals. Including the factor \(1/4\) from the measure gives the one-saddle amplitude
\[
 A_n(s)=\frac{2048}{4(2\pi)^{15/2}}
        \frac{p(\zeta)^{16n}}{(n v(\zeta))^{45/2}}.              \tag{4.1}
\]
The square root and power are chosen continuously with \(\Re v(\zeta)>0\). The negative saddle contributes the complex conjugate.

Let \(S_j=\sum_i\eta_i^j\) and \(Q=\sum_\nu D_\nu^2\). The first correction to the Gaussian integrand is
\[
 -\frac Q{12}+\frac{n\kappa_4(\zeta)}{24}S_4
       -\frac{n^2\kappa_3(\zeta)^2}{72}S_3^2.                 \tag{4.2}
\]
The relevant normalized unit-scale weighted Gaussian moments are
\[
 \mathbb EQ=192,\qquad \mathbb ES_4=5055/16,
 \qquad \mathbb ES_3^2=2835/4.
\]
They yield
\[
 B_1(s)=-\frac{16}{v(\zeta)}
  +\frac{1685\kappa_4(\zeta)}{128v(\zeta)^2}
  -\frac{315\kappa_3(\zeta)^2}{32v(\zeta)^3}.                 \tag{4.3}
\]
Equations (3.5)–(3.6) imply
\[
 \left|\frac{B_1(s)}n\right|
 \le\frac{2173895}{41154V}<\frac{53}{V}.                     \tag{4.4}
\]
No approximation to the zeros is inferred from this formal correction alone. The next two sections supply actual remainder bounds.

## 5. A uniform local remainder

Let
\[
 \mathcal B=\{\eta\in\mathcal T:|\eta_i|\le2/5\text{ for all }i\}.
\]
We compare the exact integral over this box to the Gaussian plus (4.2) over the **same** box. The resulting one-saddle error is
\[
 \left|I_{\mathcal B}-I_{\mathcal B}^{(1)}\right|
 \le \frac{36821}{V^2}|A_n(s)|,\qquad V\ge100.                 \tag{5.1}
\]
Here the factors \(p(\zeta)^{16n}\) and angular normalization are included in both integrals.

### 5.1. An even homotopy

Put
\[
 L(\eta)=\sum_i\{\log p(\zeta e^{i\eta_i})-\log p(\zeta)\},
 \quad E_\tau=nL(\tau\eta)/\tau^2,
\]
\[
 F_\tau=\prod_\nu\operatorname{sinc}^2(\tau D_\nu/2),
 \qquad J(\tau,\eta)=H F_\tau e^{E_\tau}.
\]
All singularities at \(\tau=0\) are removable. In particular,
\(E_0=-nv(\zeta)\|\eta\|^2/2\). Since
\(J(-\tau,\eta)=J(\tau,-\eta)\), integration over the symmetric box is even in \(\tau\). Taylor's integral remainder therefore gives exactly
\[
 \int_{\mathcal B}\{J(1)-J(0)-\tfrac12J''(0)\}
 =\int_0^1\frac{(1-\tau)^3}{6}
       \int_{\mathcal B}\partial_\tau^4J(\tau,\eta)\,d\eta\,d\tau.
                                                               \tag{5.2}
\]
Parity is used only in this integrated identity. Odd terms in the fourth derivative cannot be discarded at nonzero \(\tau\), because \(e^{E_\tau}\) need not be even in \(\eta\).

### 5.2. Bounds for the fourth derivative

For \(m\ge1\), Taylor's integral formula gives
\[
 \partial_\tau^m E_\tau
 =n\sum_i(i\eta_i)^{m+2}
       \int_0^1 u^m(1-u)
       \kappa_{m+2}(\zeta e^{iu\tau\eta_i})\,du.                \tag{5.3}
\]
Set \(d=2/5\), \(A=|S_3|\), \(B=S_4\), \(C=|S_5|\), \(D_6=S_6\). The scalar bounds imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 e_1&=V\{A/5+(\tau/9+9d\tau^2/160+8d^2\tau^3/45)B\},\\
 e_2&=V(1/9+9d\tau/80+8d^2\tau^2/15)B,\\
 e_3&=V(9C/80+16\tau D_6/15),\\
 e_4&=16VD_6/15,
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{5.4}
\]
with \(|\partial_\tau^mE_\tau|\le e_m\). In the first two lines only, use \(|S_5|\le dS_4\), \(S_6\le d^2S_4\).

The identity \(\operatorname{sinc}^2(t/2)=\mathbb E e^{it(U+U')}\), for independent uniforms on \([-1/2,1/2]\), gives
\[
 |F|\le1,\quad |F'|\le\tau Q/6=f_1,\quad |F''|\le Q/6=f_2,
\]
\[
 |F'''|\le\tau Q^2/12=f_3,\qquad |F''''|\le Q^2/12=f_4.         \tag{5.5}
\]
For example, the exact fourth moment is \(Q^2/12-\sum D_\nu^4/60\), so dropping the last term gives an upper bound. The odd derivative estimates follow by integrating the next derivative from zero.

The product rule now gives
\[
 |J^{(4)}|\le H e^{\Re E_\tau}\mathcal P,
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P={}&f_4+4f_3e_1+6f_2(e_2+e_1^2)
 +4f_1(e_3+3e_1e_2+e_1^3)\\
 &+e_4+4e_1e_3+3e_2^2+6e_1^2e_2+e_1^4.                       \tag{5.6}
\end{aligned}
\]
This polynomial has nonnegative coefficients in its nonnegative arguments.

### 5.3. Gaussian domination and a reproducible finite moment sum

Real-part Taylor bounds give
\[
 \Re E_\tau\le-\frac n2a\|\eta\|^2,
 \qquad a=\beta-\frac4{75}v_0\ge\frac{269}{300}v_0
                         >\left(\frac{15}{16}\right)^2v_0.
\]
The ratio of this real Gaussian normalization to the absolute complex Gaussian normalization is
\[
 (|v(\zeta)|/a)^{45/2}<2(285/269)^{45/2}<8.                  \tag{5.7}
\]
Under the normalized \(H\)-weighted Gaussian at unit scale, the orthonormal Haar coordinates are independent with density
\(x^2e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\). Hence
\[
 \mathbb ED_\nu^{2r}=(2r+1)!!m_\nu^r,
 \qquad \mathbb ED_\nu^{2r+1}=0.                              \tag{5.8}
\]
The reconstruction
\[
 \eta_i=\sum_{\nu\text{ ancestor of }i}
       \frac{\epsilon_{i,\nu}D_\nu}{m_\nu},\qquad
 \epsilon_{i,\nu}\in\{-1,1\},                                \tag{5.9}
\]
makes every moment needed in (5.6) a finite rational sum. Absolute odd powers of \(S_3\) or \(S_5\) are bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz, moving one odd factor to each side so that both resulting powers are even. An integer ceiling of the resulting square root is used. For example,
\[
 \mathbb ES_3^4=38064735/16,\quad
 \mathbb ES_5^2=81661725/256,\quad
 \mathbb ES_4^4=2166032330330625/65536.
\]

For a monomial \(C_0\tau^pV^lA^aB^bC^cD_6^dQ^e\) in (5.6), let \(q=3a+4b+5c+6d+2e\) denote its angular degree. Every monomial has \(q-2l\ge4\). Its contribution to the coefficient of \(V^{-2}\), for \(V\ge100\), is bounded by
\[
 8\,\frac{p!}{(p+4)!}\,
 [\text{monomial coefficient}]\,[\text{unit moment bound}]
 \left(\frac{16}{15}\right)^q10^{2l-q+4}.                    \tag{5.10}
\]
Here \(p!/(p+4)!\) is the exact integral of \(\tau^p(1-\tau)^3/6\); replacing all these weights by \(1/24\) is unnecessarily wasteful.

Expanding the explicit polynomial (5.6), applying (5.8)–(5.10), and adding rational upper bounds gives
\[
 \frac{92329284077596045529773584373060993024}
 {2507581295866705477237701416015625}<36821.
\]
This proves (5.1). The moment calculation is finite and exact; it is not an asymptotic assertion with an unspecified constant.

### 5.4. Restoring the Gaussian model outside the box

The exact leaf-coordinate moment-generating function under the normalized real weighted Gaussian is
\[
 \mathbb E e^{t\sqrt{n\beta}\eta_i}
 =e^{15t^2/32}\prod_{m=2,4,8,16}(1+t^2/m).                    \tag{5.11}
\]
Indeed a leaf has four ancestor Haar coordinates with squared coefficients \(1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16\). A union and Chernoff bound therefore give
\[
 P_{\mathcal B^c}\le32
 e^{-td\sqrt{19V/20}+15t^2/32}
 \prod_{m=2,4,8,16}(1+t^2/m).                                \tag{5.12}
\]
The absolute value of (4.2) is at most
\[
 Q/12+(V/18)S_4+(V^2/50)S_3^2.
\]
Its weighted \(L^2\) norm is bounded by \(67/V\); the exact squared constant is
\(7405704307825/1693651716<67^2\). Cauchy–Schwarz and the conditioning factor in (3.5) bound the restored Gaussian-plus-correction tail by
\[
 2\{P_{\mathcal B^c}+(67/V)\sqrt{P_{\mathcal B^c}}\}|A_n(s)|.
\]
For \(V\ge225\), choose \(t=5\) in (5.12). Exact rational exponential and square-root bounds give
\[
 P_{\mathcal B^c}<1/1000,\qquad
 2\{P_{\mathcal B^c}+(67/V)\sqrt{P_{\mathcal B^c}}\}<11/500.
                                                               \tag{5.13}
\]
These are tails of the Gaussian model. They are not substituted for the actual torus complement, which is bounded next.

## 6. The actual torus complement at \(V\ge250\)

Represent each angle in \([-\pi,\pi]\); boundary sets have measure zero. Call an angle central if \(|\alpha_i|\le\pi/3\). We divide the complement of the two local boxes into five classes. All bounds in this section are relative to the magnitude of **one** saddle, \(|A_n(s)|\).

### 6.1. Principal central sectors outside the boxes

In the sector \(\sum\alpha_i=\pi\), set \(\eta_i=\alpha_i-\theta\), and define
\[
 g(x)=\frac{h_s(\theta)+h_s'(\theta)x-h_s(\theta+x)}{v_0}.
\]
For either radial direction, the scalar inequalities imply at \(d=2/5\)
\[
 g(\pm d)\ge269/3750,\qquad
 \frac d{dr}g(\pm r)\big|_{r=d}\ge43/125.
\]
These follow by integrating
\(g''(0)\ge19/20\), \(|g'''(0)|\le1/4\), and \(g''''\ge-3/2\).
For larger radial distances still inside the central angular interval, (3.4) gives \(g''\ge1/3\). Thus
\[
 g(\pm r)\ge269/3750+(43/125)(r-d)+(r-d)^2/6\quad(r\ge d).
\]
Let
\[
 \gamma(R)=\frac13+\frac{158}{375R}-\frac{49}{625R^2}.
\]
It is decreasing on \([2/5,5/4]\), and \(g(x)\ge\gamma(R)x^2/2\) whenever \(|x|\le R\). The largest possible \(|\eta_i|\) in this sector is \(19\pi/48<5/4\).

Split the maximum coordinate into the seventeen slabs
\[
 [\ell_j,R_j]=[2/5+j/20,\,2/5+(j+1)/20],\qquad0\le j\le16.
\]
On a slab, use \(K\le H\) and the Gaussian precision \(\gamma(R_j)V\). Its normalization costs at most
\(2(17/(16\gamma(R_j)))^{23}\). Equation (5.11) gives the explicit slab bound
\[
 64\left(\frac{17}{16\gamma(R_j)}\right)^{23}
 e^{-t_j\ell_j\sqrt{\gamma(R_j)V}+15t_j^2/32}
 \prod_{m=2,4,8,16}(1+t_j^2/m).                               \tag{6.1}
\]
At \(V=250\), take a rational lower bound for the square root by flooring it to denominator \(1000\), and set \(t_j\) equal to the integer part of the resulting \(\ell_j\sqrt{250\gamma(R_j)}\), minus one, or one if this is smaller. Sum (6.1), bounding each negative exponential by the reciprocal of a positive exponential Taylor partial sum. The exact sum is less than \(9/80\). Each term decreases for larger \(V\). Thus the two principal central complements together contribute less than \(9/40\).

### 6.2. Nonprincipal central sectors

The other central sectors have means \(\mu=\pm3\theta,\pm5\theta\). Strong concavity gives
\[
 \sum_i h_s(\alpha_i)
 \le16h_s(\mu)-\frac{v_0}{6}\sum_i(\alpha_i-\mu)^2.
\]
The wide fourth-cumulant bound implies
\[
 h_s(\theta)-h_s(3\theta)
 \ge v_0(4\theta^2-10\theta^4)>v_0/8.
\]
For example, integrate \(\Re v(se^{ia})\ge v_0(1-3a^2/2)\) first from zero to \(a\), then from \(\theta\) to \(3\theta\). The larger mean \(5\theta\) only improves the loss. There are sixteen leaves, so the total mean deficit exceeds \(2v_0\). The four sectors together are bounded by
\[
 8(51/16)^{23}e^{-2V}<10^{-10},\qquad V\ge250.                 \tag{6.2}
\]

### 6.3. Exactly one noncentral angle

Let its magnitude be \(a\in[\pi/3,\pi]\). From (3.1),
\[
 h_s(a)\le-v_0\phi(a),\qquad
 \phi(a)=\frac{2-\cos a-\cos^2a}{3}.
\]
For \(|x|\le2\pi/45\), (3.7) and Taylor's formula give
\[
 h_s(x)\le-v_0x^2/2+v_0x^4/16\le-(49/100)v_0x^2.
\]
The mean of the fifteen inliers has magnitude at least \((\pi-a)/15\). Monotonicity of \(h_s\), followed by the short-angle estimate at this smaller comparison argument, gives a mean cost at least \((49/1500)v_0(\pi-a)^2\). The remaining inlier fluctuations have Gaussian precision \(V/3\).

The function \(\phi(a)+(49/1500)(\pi-a)^2\) is bounded below by
\(5/12+49\pi^2/3375\). To check this, it is concave on \([\pi/3,2\pi/3]\), because \(\phi''\le-1/6\) while the added second derivative is \(49/750<1/6\); its first endpoint is the smaller one. On \([2\pi/3,\pi]\), \(\phi\ge2/3\), which is larger than that endpoint value. After subtracting (3.3), the deficit exceeds
\[
 \delta_1=\frac{3246499}{13837500},                            \tag{6.3}
\]
using \(\pi^2>493/50\).

Only the four ancestors of the outer leaf need their kernel factors bounded by two. The eleven remaining contrasts lie orthogonally in the fourteen-dimensional centered inlier space, and their product of \(m_\nu/2\) is \(32\). Their Gaussian integral is
\(32(2\pi)^7(3/V)^{18}\). The original measure disintegrates as \(da\,d\sigma/\sqrt{15}\). There are at most sixteen choices of leaf and six winding sectors. After normalizing by (4.1), the total is bounded by
\[
 128\,3^{18}(17/16)^{23}V^{9/2}e^{-\delta_1V}<1/2000,
 \qquad V\ge250.                                             \tag{6.4}
\]
The factor uses \(\sqrt{2\pi/15}<2/3\) and includes the conditioning factor two.

### 6.4. Exactly two noncentral angles

The two leaf-to-root paths have at most seven distinct internal ancestors. At least eight contrasts therefore involve only the fourteen central leaves. Keep eight such contrasts and bound the other seven kernel factors by two. Each retained node has size at most eight, so
\[
 K\le2^{23}\prod_{j=1}^8x_j^2
\]
in orthonormal coordinates of the thirteen-dimensional centered inlier plane. Its Gaussian integral contributes \((2\pi)^{13/2}(3/V)^{29/2}\). The coarea factor is \(1/\sqrt{14}\), and there are at most \(\binom{16}{2}\cdot6=720\) leaf-pair/winding choices.

The deficit relative to the saddle is at least
\(2(5/12)-40/123=125/246\). Including the conditioning factor two, the total bound is
\[
 720\,2^{17}3^{14}(17/16)^{23}V^8e^{-125V/246}<10^{-10},
 \qquad V\ge250.                                             \tag{6.5}
\]
Keeping the eight intact contrasts is essential: replacing the whole kernel by a constant would introduce a much larger power of \(V\).

### 6.5. At least three noncentral angles

Here the deficit is at least
\(3(5/12)-40/123=455/492\). Use \(K\le2^{15}\) and normalized total torus volume one. The resulting bound is
\[
 128(44/7)^8(17/16)^{23}V^{23}e^{-455V/492}<10^{-10},
 \qquad V\ge250.                                             \tag{6.6}
\]
No union multiplier is necessary: this is a pointwise bound on the entire region.

All numerical inequalities (6.1)–(6.6) are checked by rational arithmetic, positive exponential Taylor sums and elementary rational bounds on \(\pi\). The functions \(V^a e^{-\delta V}\) in (6.4)–(6.6) decrease for \(V\ge250\), as follows by differentiating their logarithms.

## 7. The uniform bulk theorem and phase points

Combining the two local errors, the two model-restoration errors, the actual complement, and (4.4), we obtain
\[
 \frac{|f_{n,4}(-s^{16})-2\Re A_n(s)|}{2|A_n(s)|}
 <\frac{36821}{250^2}+\frac{53}{250}+\frac{11}{500}
    +\frac12\left(\frac9{40}+\frac1{2000}+\frac3{10^{10}}\right)
 =\frac{18717720003}{20000000000}<\frac{47}{50}                 \tag{7.1}
\]
whenever \(0<s\le1\) and \(V\ge250\). Notice that the actual complement bounds were relative to one saddle; this accounts for the factor \(1/2\) in the last term.

Define
\[
 \Psi_n(s)=16n\arg p(\zeta)-\frac{45}{2}\arg v(\zeta).
\]
Let \(m_q=zq'/q\). Direct algebra gives, for \(z=se^{i\theta}\),
\[
 \Im m(z)=\frac{s\sin\theta(1+4s\cos\theta+s^2)}{|p(z)|^2}>0,
\]
\[
 \Im m_q(z)=\frac{4s\sin\theta(1+s\cos\theta+s^2)}{|q(z)|^2}
             \le4\Im m(z).
\]
The last inequality uses
\(|q|^2-|p|^2=6s\cos\theta(1+s^2)+15s^2\ge0\). Since
\(zv'/v=1+m_q-2m\),
\[
 \frac{d\Psi_n}{d\log s}\ge(16n-45)\Im m(z)>0\qquad(n\ge3).  \tag{7.2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \Psi_n(0+)=-45\pi/32,\qquad \Psi_n(1)=n\pi.
\]
Thus every integer phase level \(m\pi\), \(0\le m\le n\), has a unique point in \((0,1]\). We use only levels \(m\ge250\), not formal phase crossings near the endpoint.

For \(n\ge1001\), let \(s_*\) solve \(nv(s_*)=250\). The real variance is strictly increasing on \((0,1)\), since
\[
 sv'(s)=\frac{s(1-s^2)(1+7s+s^2)}{p(s)^3}>0.
\]
The exact value \(v(7/20)=141260/346921\) shows \(s_*<7/20\). On this interval,
\[
 \frac{m(s)}{v(s)}-1=\frac{s(2s^2+2s-1)}{1+4s+s^2}\le0.
\]
Also, for \(0\le\alpha\le\theta\),
\[
 \Im v(se^{i\alpha})=
 \frac{s\sin\alpha(1-s^2)
 (4s^2\cos^2\alpha+8s^3\cos\alpha+8s\cos\alpha+s^4+5s^2+1)}
 {|p(se^{i\alpha})|^4}\ge0.
\]
Therefore \(\arg p(se^{i\alpha})\) is concave in \(\alpha\), and
\(\arg p(\zeta)\le\theta m(s)\), while \(\arg v(\zeta)\ge0\). Hence
\[
 \Psi_n(s_*)\le16n\theta v(s_*)=250\pi.                      \tag{7.3}
\]
By monotonicity, all phase points \(s_{n,m}\), \(250\le m\le n\), have \(V\ge250\). At such a point, \(\Re A_n=(-1)^m|A_n|\), so (7.1) proves
\[
 (-1)^m f_{n,4}(-s_{n,m}^{16})>0,
 \qquad250\le m\le n,\quad n\ge1001.                         \tag{7.4}
\]
In particular, \(s_{n,n}=1\) and \(f_{n,4}(-1)\ne0\).

## 8. The analytic edge family and exact enclosure lemma

### 8.1. A family regular at \(u=0\)

For complex \(u,z\) with \(|uz|<1\), define
\[
 g_u(z)=\exp L(u,z),\qquad
 L(u,z)=\begin{cases}\log p(uz)/u,&u\ne0,\\z,&u=0.\end{cases} 
\]
The logarithm is its analytic branch at zero. The displayed singularity is removable. Set
\[
 b_{0,k}(u)=[z^k]g_u(z),\qquad b_{j+1,k}=b_{j,k}^2-b_{j,k-1}b_{j,k+1},
 \qquad F_u(x)=\sum_{k\ge0}b_{4,k}(u)x^k.                     \tag{8.1}
\]
These analytic functions are used only on domains where the bounds below ensure convergence. For a reciprocal integer \(u=1/n\),
\[
 b_{0,k}(1/n)=n^{-k}a_{n,k},\qquad
 F_{1/n}(x)=f_{n,4}(x/n^{16}).                               \tag{8.2}
\]
The second identity follows because one application of \(\mathcal L\) squares a geometric coefficient scaling. It is exact, not an asymptotic limit.

### 8.2. Complex-parameter majorant

The series
\[
 \log p(w)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}c_\ell w^\ell,\qquad
 c_\ell=\begin{cases}1/\ell,&3\nmid\ell,\\-2/\ell,&3\mid\ell,\end{cases}
\]
follows from \(p(w)=(1-w^3)/(1-w)\). Put
\[
 G(q)=-\log(1-q)-\tfrac13\log(1-q^3),
 \qquad H(q)=\frac{qG'(q)-G(q)}{q^2}.
\]
Let \(c\ge0\), \(r,R>0\), and \(q=(c+r)R<1\). On \(|u-c|\le r\), \(|z|\le R\),
\[
 |\partial_uL(u,z)|\le R^2H(q).
\]
Integrating from \(c\) to \(u\) gives
\[
 |g_u(z)|\le e^E,\qquad
 E=\frac{\log p(cR)}c+rR^2H(q),                              \tag{8.3}
\]
where the quotient is interpreted as \(R\) when \(c=0\). At the real starting parameter \(c>0\), the bound follows from \(|p(cz)|\le p(cR)\).

Cauchy's coefficient estimate followed by four applications of \(\mathcal L\) yields
\[
 |b_{4,k}(u)|\le2^{15}e^{16E}R^{-16k}.                       \tag{8.4}
\]
Indeed the constants obey \(C_{j+1}=2C_j^2\), \(C_0=1\), while the spatial exponent doubles at every layer. A missing negative-index neighbor only improves the estimate.

### 8.3. Moving-node Taylor remainder

Let a positive rational moving node on a real panel \([c-h,c+h]\) be
\[
 t(u)=\frac{t_c}{1+a(u-c)},\qquad |a|r<1,\qquad0<h<r.
\]
Then \(|t(u)|\le T=t_c/(1-|a|r)\) throughout the **complex** parameter disk. Choose \(R>T\) with \((c+r)R<1\). For the truncated evaluation
\[
 Q_K(u)=\sum_{k=0}^K b_{4,k}(u)(-t(u)^{16})^k,
\]
(8.4) gives
\[
 |Q_K(u)|\le M=\frac{2^{15}e^{16E}}{1-(T/R)^{16}}.
\]
If \(Q_K(c+hw)=\sum_{j\ge0}d_jw^j\), Cauchy's estimate proves
\[
 \left|Q_K(c+hw)-\sum_{j=0}^{d}d_jw^j\right|
 \le M\frac{(h/r)^{d+1}}{1-h/r},\qquad |w|\le1.              \tag{8.5}
\]
The finite coefficient truncation is bounded separately. For real \(0\le u\le U\),
\[
 \log p(uR_0)/u\le R_0+UR_0^2/2=:E_0,
\]
because \(\log p(q)\le q+q^2/2\). If \(R_0>T_0=\max t(u)\) and \(UR_0<1\), then
\[
 |F_u(-t(u)^{16})-Q_K(u)|
 \le\frac{2^{15}e^{16E_0}(T_0/R_0)^{16(K+1)}}
             {1-(T_0/R_0)^{16}}.                            \tag{8.6}
\]
The complex-disk radius in (8.5) need not equal the real coefficient-tail radius in (8.6). In the certificates, \(r=4h\), \(R=T+1\), and \(R_0=2T_0\) are used after checking the displayed domain inequalities exactly.

### 8.4. Exact finite Taylor arithmetic and the sign test

The retained Taylor coefficients are enclosed as
\[
 d_i\in2^{e-P}[m_i-\rho,m_i+\rho],\qquad0\le i\le d,
\]
with integer midpoints, a common nonnegative integer radius, and an arbitrary integer binary exponent. Thus rounding contributes at most
\((d+1)\rho2^{e-P}\) on \(|w|\le1\).

For multiplication, exact integer convolution of the midpoint vectors has raw coefficient error at most
\[
 \rho_b\sum|m_a|+\rho_a\sum|m_b|+(d+1)\rho_a\rho_b.
\]
Its raw exponent is \(e_a+e_b-P\). Rescaling a midpoint by a positive integer denominator \(D\), with nearest integer \(n_i\), enlarges the radius to
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{\rho+\max_i|Dn_i-m_i|}{D}\right\rceil.
\]
These formulas prove the enclosure invariant independently of intermediate coefficient size. Normalization uses both midpoint magnitudes and the radius; a zero midpoint with nonzero radius is not discarded.
For multiplication by a rational scalar \(N/D\), \(D>0\), choose integer rounded midpoints \(n_i\); a valid new common radius is
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{|N|\rho+\max_i|Dn_i-Nm_i|}{D}\right\rceil.
\]
Addition first aligns binary exponents by these outward-rounded rescalings and then adds the radii. A formal coefficient shift preserves the common radius on retained entries. Thus all primitive operations used in the recurrence preserve the stated coefficientwise enclosure.


The initial jets are generated from the exact recurrence
\[
 b_{0,0}=b_{0,1}=1,\qquad
 (k+1)b_{0,k+1}=(1-ku)b_{0,k}+u(2-(k-1)u)b_{0,k-1}.           \tag{8.7}
\]
Four radius-one stencils give the shared \(b_{4,k}\) jets. At each moving node, Horner evaluation uses the Taylor jet
\[
 -t_c^{16}(1+ahw)^{-16},\qquad
 [w^j]=-t_c^{16}\binom{15+j}{j}(-ah)^j.
\]
Truncating after formal degree \(d\) cannot affect retained formal coefficients.

Convert the midpoint polynomial to Bernstein form after \(w=2x-1\), \(0\le x\le1\). Its minimum and maximum controls enclose all values. Add the rounding envelope and the two independent errors (8.5)–(8.6). A strictly positive lower bound or strictly negative upper bound then certifies the sign on the entire closed panel. This is an interval-wide proof, not a sampled evaluation.

All transcendental majorants can be replaced by rational upper bounds. For example, expand \(H(q)\) through degree thirty and bound the remainder by \(2q^{31}/(1-q)\); bound logarithms by the positive atanh series with its geometric tail; use a positive rational lower bound for \(\log2\) to replace \(e^X\) by \(2^{\lceil X/\log2_{\rm lower}\rceil}\). Consequently the final sign comparison is an exact rational inequality.

## 9. Parameter-wide bulk bypass and the final join

For real \(r>0\), the analytic function \(p(z)^r\) on \(|z|<1\) is defined with the logarithm at zero. The diagonal identity, kernel symmetrization and all modulus estimates remain valid: integrality of \(r\) was not used in those steps. Thus (7.1) extends to real \(r\), \(0<s<1\), \(rv(s)\ge250\). No polynomial degree or reciprocal-root assertion is made for noninteger \(r\).

At a moving node, set \(r=1/u\), \(s=ut(u)\). If
\[
 rv(s)\ge250,\qquad |\Psi_r(s)-j\pi|\le1/3,
\]
then (7.1) gives the desired sign because
\(\cos(1/3)\ge17/18>47/50\). At \(u=0\), the analytic family tends uniformly on compact sets to its Poisson-scale limit \(g_0(z)=e^z\). The amplitude has a finite positive limiting magnitude; the strict uniform margin therefore extends the sign to that endpoint.

These bypass inequalities and the final join are themselves low-degree rational polynomial certificates. Write \(t=A/B\), \(B>0\), and let
\(c=\cos\theta,d=\sin\theta,c_2=\cos2\theta,d_2=\sin2\theta\). Then
\[
 \arg p(ut e^{i\theta})=\arctan\frac{uN_p}{D_p},
\quad D_p=B^2+cuAB+c_2u^2A^2,
\quad N_p=dAB+d_2uA^2,
\]
with \(D_q=B^2+4cuAB+c_2u^2A^2\), \(N_q=4dAB+d_2uA^2\) for \(q\). Hence
\[
 \Psi_{1/u}(ut)
 =(16/u+45)\arctan(uN_p/D_p)
 -(45/2)\arctan(uN_q/D_q)-45\pi/32.                          \tag{9.1}
\]
All real parts are positive, so the branches are unambiguous. Use certified rational sine, cosine and \(\pi\) enclosures, together with
\[
 x-x^3/3\le\arctan x\le x-x^3/3+x^5/5\qquad(x\ge0).
\]
The upper polynomial is increasing; the lower is increasing on \([0,1]\), which is verified for the relevant ratio bounds. After division by \(u\), the apparent singularity in (9.1) is removable. Clearing positive denominators gives degree-sixteen polynomial phase margins. The variance condition is the degree-four inequality obtained from
\[
 \frac{v(ut)}u=
 \frac{AB(B^2+4uAB+u^2A^2)}{(B^2+uAB+u^2A^2)^2}.              \tag{9.2}
\]
Positive Bernstein controls certify both types of margin on each whole panel.

For the supplied final harmonic node, the exact inequalities establish
\(\Psi_{1/u}(ut_{249}(u))<250\pi\). For the root argument we use this only at reciprocal integers \(u=1/n\): phase monotonicity then gives
\[
 t_{249}(1/n)<n s_{n,250}.                                   \tag{9.3}
\]

 t_j(u)=\left(\frac{1-\lambda}{T_{j,\ell}}

## 11. Reproducibility boundary

The mathematical proof requires more than a floating plot or a list of successful samples. Its finite data are:

- the rational Bernstein certificates for the scalar inequalities in §3;
- the exact weighted-moment sum in §5;
- the rational complement bounds in §6;
- the frozen moving-node endpoint table, interval-wide sign enclosures, and final-join controls;
- the finite cone and PF4 certificates described in the companion chapter.

The computation uses reviewed exact integer/rational arithmetic and outward-rounded coefficient enclosures. An executable certificate checker must verify domains, strict sign inequalities, index coverage and the connection between every saved enclosure and its parameters. Cryptographic hashes help identify the intended records; hashing alone does not establish any mathematical inequality. This draft presents a computer-assisted proof, not a claim of formal proof-assistant verification or external peer review.
