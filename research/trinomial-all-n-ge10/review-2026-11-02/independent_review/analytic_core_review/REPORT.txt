The Gaussian exponent is 13/2+8=29/2. Fixing two signed angles gives coarea 1/sqrt(14), their domain area is at most (2pi)^2, and there are at most C(16,2)*6=720 leaf-pair/sector choices. BEFORE conditioning, division by one saddle gives
\[
720\,2^{14}\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{14}}\,3^{29/2}R^{45/2}V^8e^{-125V/246}.
\]
Using R^(45/2)<2(17/16)^23 and pi*sqrt(3/14)<2 yields exactly
\[
720\,2^{17}3^{14}(17/16)^{23}V^8e^{-125V/246}<10^{-10}.
\]
The endpoint check at V=250 is rational, and 8/V-125/246<0 there and afterward. This verifies (6.5). In particular the conditioning factor two cannot be omitted when passing between these two displayed expressions.

With at least three external angles, every remaining angle still has h<=0, the deficit is at least 455/492, and K<=2^15. Use normalized total torus volume one, with no leaf-choice or sector multiplier. The direct one-saddle ratio is at most 64(2pi)^(15/2)R^(45/2)V^(45/2)exp(-455V/492). \]

## 11. Assembly of (7.1), phase points, and real-exponent extension

Source: manuscript p.16. Split the exact torus integral into the two local boxes and their actual complement. At each box, first replace the exact integral by Gaussian plus first correction ON THE SAME BOX, paying at most 36821|A|/V^2. Then restore that model to the full tangent space, paying less than (11/500)|A|. Its value is A*(1+B1/n). The negative-saddle expressions are conjugates.

The two first-correction terms together have modulus at most 2|A|*53/V. The actual torus complement is a different error and is added only once. Dividing the total error by 2|A| cancels the factor two on local/model/correction errors and divides the one-saddle complement bound by two. Thus
\[
\frac{|f_{n,4}(-s^{16})-2\mathrm{Re}\,A_n(s)|}{2|A_n(s)|}
\]
\[
<\frac{36821}{250^2}+\frac{53}{250}+\frac{11}{500}+\frac12\left(\frac9{40}+\frac1{2000}+\frac3{10^{10}}\right)=\frac{18717720003}{20000000000}.
\]
The strict margin below 47/50 is 82279997/20000000000. Every term controls a precisely identified part of the exact integral. There is neither a Gaussian-for-torus substitution nor an uncovered transition region. This completes the accepted proof of (7.1).

For the phase consequences, choose arg(v(zeta)) in the right half-plane, and define Psi=16n arg(p(zeta))-(45/2)arg(v(zeta)). Put m=z*p'/p and m_q=z*q'/q with q=1+4z+z^2. Direct algebra gives positive Im(m), and Im(m_q)<=4 Im(m): its numerator contains 1+s cos(theta)+s^2 instead of the larger 1+4s cos(theta)+s^2, while |q|^2>=|p|^2. Hence
\[
\frac{d\Psi_n}{d\log s}\ge(16n-45)\mathrm{Im}\,m(\zeta)>0\quad(n\ge3).
\]
At s down to zero, arg p tends to zero and arg v tends to theta, giving Psi(0+)=-45pi/32. At s=1, p(exp(i theta))=exp(i theta)(1+2cos theta), and v(exp(i theta))=(4+2cos theta)/(1+2cos theta)^2 is positive real. Thus Psi(1)=n*pi with no hidden branch multiple.

For n>=1001, the following exact identities prove the threshold bridge:
\[
s v'(s)=\frac{s(1-s^2)(1+7s+s^2)}{p(s)^3},\qquad \frac{m(s)}{v(s)}-1=\frac{s(2s^2+2s-1)}{1+4s+s^2}.
\]
Thus v(s) increases strictly on (0,1), and 1001*v(7/20)>250; the unique s_star with n*v(s_star)=250 lies below 7/20, where m(s)<=v(s). For 0<=alpha<=theta put c=cos(alpha); direct algebra gives
\[
\mathrm{Im}\,v(se^{i\alpha})=\frac{s\sin\alpha(1-s^2)(4s^2c^2+8s^3c+8sc+s^4+5s^2+1)}{|p(se^{i\alpha})|^4}\ge0.
\]
This nonnegative numerator makes arg p concave in alpha, so arg p(zeta)<=theta*m(s), while arg v(zeta)>=0. Therefore Psi(s_star)<=250*pi. Phase monotonicity places every integer phase j*pi with 250<=j<=n in V>=250. At those points the accepted estimate gives (-1)^j*f(n,4)(-s_j^16)>0, including a nonzero value at -1. The six polynomial identities used here are independently checked by local/verify_phase_identities.py, and the detailed argument is in local/PHASE_AND_REAL_EXPONENT.txt.

For real nu>0 and 0<s<1, p has no zero in the open unit disk and has a unique analytic logarithm normalized at zero. The coefficients of p^nu are real. On each compact smaller disk their series converges absolutely; each of four quadratic coefficient transforms has radius at least one. The nested constant-term integrals at radii s therefore converge absolutely and the same sixteen-leaf identity holds. Permutation symmetry, conjugation, modulus estimates, and local derivatives all persist. The proof depends on nu only through positive V and multiplicative derivatives, not integrality. This proves the stated analytic extension. Degree exhaustion and reciprocal root counting are not used for noninteger nu.

## 14. Three separate priority questions

Full target. Medina and Straub [S1], printed p.11, Conjecture 5.2 and Example 5.3, distinguish the general positive-coefficient convolution-power conjecture from the exact trinomial target. The latter is an explicitly posed problem. The original PDF says lambda>=10 (not lambda>10) and reports a proof for 10<=lambda<=500. The integer interpretation comes from its polynomial/convolution-power context, not an extra explicit integer quantifier in that sentence. The sources examined do not supply a ready-made proof covering every integer n>=10, but absence of a search hit is not evidence of priority or an exhaustive proof of present openness. The full target's final priority remains UNDETERMINED.

Fourth-layer structure. The assertion that f(n,4) has only negative real zeros for all n>=4004 is stronger than the corresponding infinite log-concavity conclusion. Branden [S2], Section 3, preserves real-rootedness once it is present; it does not create it from the nonreal roots of p^n. The Xie-Zhang-Zhang curvature criteria [S3], Theorem 2.3, Corollary 2.6 and Theorems 8.1-8.2, concern positivity and stopping times, not real-rootedness of a fixed layer; their application to the trinomial family would itself require substantial new work. No direct covering theorem was identified in the checked sources. Priority remains UNDETERMINED; 4004 is an effective proof threshold, not a proven optimal threshold.

Finite-layer entry into the real-rooted class is not itself a new general strategy. Xie and Philip Zhang [S4], p.3 Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2, give a public proof that the first transform of Boros-Moll rows has simple negative roots, then invoke Branden. Their Narayana/Abel-summation method and sequence are different; this is a method precedent, not coverage of the trinomial theorem. This review checked the cited statements and reduction, not every proof detail of that separate recent preprint.

Sixteen-leaf method. Fill, Flajolet and Kapur [S5], author-version p.21 equations (37)-(38), explicitly state the classical Hadamard contour integral for coefficientwise products. Iterating the quadratic transform naturally yields a binary tree; sixteen leaves mean fifteen integration variables, not sixteen. The finite child-swap averaging and Gaussian moments are classical operations. The title 'Tree Recurrences' in [S5] concerns algorithmic/random-tree recurrences and must not be mistaken for the present log-concavity tree.

Pemantle and Wilson [S6], p.5 Theorem 2.3, pp.6-7 Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.4, and p.8 Theorem 4.1, provide explicit precedents for complex-phase multivariate saddle expansions, sums of saddle contributions, and vanishing amplitudes. Those fixed-phase asymptotics do not directly supply uniform explicit constants as s approaches zero with V=n*v(s)>=250. Nor do they by themselves establish all roots, where the two conjugate leading terms can cancel. The potentially substantive method contribution is the effective parameter-uniform control for this tree kernel and its application to the full root problem. The precise tree-kernel formula's first publication has not been finally established.

Access limits. The full Csordas 2011 chapter 'Iterated Turan Inequalities and a Conjecture of P. Branden', DOI 10.1007/978-3-0348-0142-3_7, was not obtained; only publisher metadata/abstract material was available. The Cardon-Rich 2008 full PDF also could not be read. No claim about absence of a specific integral formula in either unread source is made. The literature appendix lists further checked sources and the search boundary. This was not an exhaustive citation-database review or expert confirmation.
